Autoclave Troubleshooting

PROJECT STATUS: Learning / Practical Reference

An autoclave sterilizes equipment using pressurized steam at a controlled temperature and exposure time. Correct operation and maintenance help ensure effective sterilization.

How It Works

Water is heated to produce steam. The chamber reaches the required temperature and pressure for the selected cycle. After the holding period, pressure is released according to the manufacturer's procedure.

Main Components

  • Sterilization chamber and door seal
  • Heating element or steam supply
  • Temperature sensor and controller
  • Pressure gauge or pressure sensor
  • Safety valve and pressure protection system
  • Drain, water reservoir and control panel

Common Faults

  • Unit won't power on: Check the approved power supply, plug, fuse and circuit breaker.
  • Fails to heat: Possible heater, sensor, controller or power-circuit fault.
  • Pressure fails to rise: Check water level, door seal, steam leaks and the manufacturer's fault codes.
  • Water leakage: Inspect seals, hoses and connections after the unit is safely shut down.
  • Cycle doesn't complete: Record the error code and check sensor readings and the drain system according to the service manual.

Preventive Maintenance

  • Inspect the chamber, door gasket and visible connections.
  • Clean the chamber and drain as specified by the manufacturer.
  • Check safety devices and calibration according to the service schedule.
  • Document faults, repairs and maintenance results.

Safety

Never open the door while the chamber is pressurized or hot. Isolate power before electrical inspection and allow the unit to cool and depressurize. Repairs and safety-device tests should be performed by trained personnel following the service manual.

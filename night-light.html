● PLANNED / LEARNING PROJECT

Automatic Night Light

A practical electronics project exploring how a circuit can detect changes in ambient light and automatically control a low-voltage light source.

Electronics LDR Sensor Transistor Switching Prototyping

01. Project Overview

The goal is to develop a simple automatic lighting system that responds to the amount of light in its surroundings. The project will provide practical experience with sensors, electronic components, circuit assembly, and troubleshooting.

Current stage: Planning and learning. The circuit has not yet been built or tested.

02. How It Works

  1. Light detection: A light-dependent resistor (LDR) changes resistance as the surrounding light changes.
  2. Signal control: The LDR and other components form a sensing circuit that responds to the light level.
  3. Switching: A transistor or suitable switching stage controls current to the lamp or LED.
  4. Automatic response: The circuit is designed to turn the light on in darkness and off in brighter conditions.

The exact operation depends on the final circuit arrangement and component values.

03. Components and Their Functions

Light-dependent resistor

Senses changes in ambient light by changing resistance.

Fixed resistors

Help set circuit current and the sensor's switching response.

Transistor

Acts as an electronic switch to control the light.

LED or low-voltage lamp

Provides the light output when the circuit switches on.

Breadboard and jumper wires

Allow temporary circuit assembly without soldering.

Low-voltage DC supply

Provides power matched to the final circuit's requirements.

04. Planned Build Stages

  1. Study the circuit diagram and identify each component.
  2. Confirm component specifications and transistor pin connections.
  3. Assemble the circuit on a breadboard using a suitable low-voltage supply.
  4. Observe how the circuit responds to bright and dark conditions.
  5. Record results, correct faults, and document any improvements.

05. Testing and Troubleshooting Plan

  • Inspect wiring and component placement before applying power.
  • Verify supply voltage and polarity against the circuit design.
  • Check whether the LDR resistance changes with light.
  • Verify transistor type and pin connections using its datasheet.
  • Check for loose connections and incorrect resistor values.
  • Record whether the light switches at the expected conditions.

Testing will use a suitable low-voltage DC supply. Do not connect an experimental breadboard circuit directly to mains electricity.

06. Learning Objectives

  • Understand the operation of an LDR sensor.
  • Learn the basics of transistor switching.
  • Develop breadboard assembly skills.
  • Practise systematic fault diagnosis.
  • Document an electronics project from planning to testing.

07. Future Improvements

  • Add an adjustable light-sensitivity control.
  • Explore more efficient LED switching.
  • Compare the circuit's behaviour under different lighting conditions.
  • Document the completed build with photographs and measured results.
